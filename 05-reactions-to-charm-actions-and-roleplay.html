What to expect and what is expected

  • A player can expect his reaction to modify an encounter with an NPC.

    • Reaction rolls are not the only determining factor of an encounter.

    • A good reaction roll is not alike to hypnosis or a spell.

    • Favourable reactions will not change the basic outlook of a person.

      • I.e. a Bane priest with a reaction of 26 vs. a Tyr priest will still execute the other, but will be conflicted and perhaps try to turn the other to the dark side.

    • Et krænkende ord/attitude vil muligvis blive nemmere tilgivet hvis der har været et godt reaction roll – men det er stadigvæk krænkende.

    • In principle 30% of an encounter will be influenced by the reaction roll.

The DM will sometimes roleplay or provide high charm NPC, the players should take this into account in a similar manor (i.e. be influenced ~50%)

Players needs to (try to) roleplay their charm.

Between PC’s:

Charm have limited effect and PC’s should base 50% on their reaction towards what the other PC, do, mean and roleplay, and only 50% on the stats of the person. When in doubt use it as a lead for being positive or negative

Evil actions are still evil even if the person has a high charm.

Result

≤ -4: Will Hurt You (DC +20)

Intent: Active malice with immediate violent response

- Merchant: Calls the guards immediately, may assault you while doing so. Spreads word you're dangerous/a thief

- City Guard: Draws weapon and attacks on sight. Assumes you're a criminal threat

- General NPC: Acts with physical aggression, sabotage, or attempts to cause direct harm

-3 to -1: Wants to Hurt You (DC +15)

Intent: Seeks to harm you through indirect means; willing to escalate to violence

- Merchant: Adulterates your food/drink, overcharges egregiously, sells faulty goods knowingly

- City Guard: Arrests you on trumped-up charges or the flimsiest pretense

- General NPC: Spreads malicious rumors, sets traps (social or physical), actively plots against you

- DM Guidance: The NPC restrains themselves from immediate violence due to law, witnesses, or self-preservation, but they're looking for any excuse. They may provoke you into acting first

0 to 2: Hate You (DC +10)

Intent: Deep personal animosity; seeks to exclude and harm your interests

- Merchant: Refuses service entirely, slams door in face, may shout insults

- City Guard: Goes out of their way to make your life difficult within legal bounds; follows you, questions you repeatedly

- General NPC: Won't speak to you, turns their back, warns others about you

- DM Guidance: This is personal dislike that runs deep but stays within social bounds (barely). The NPC needs little excuse to make things difficult but won't risk themselves to harm you

3 to 5: Hostile (DC +5)

Intent: Active unfriendliness; looking for reasons to be difficult

- Merchant: Spits in your drink when you're not looking, gives you the worst merchandise, serves you last

- City Guard: Follows you around, watches for any minor infractions, harasses with questions

- General NPC: Speaks curtly or rudely, makes snide remarks, gives you the cold shoulder

- DM Guidance: The NPC doesn't like you and isn't hiding it well, but this is still within "professional" discourtesy. They're hoping you'll leave or give them a reason to escalate

6 to 8: Dislike (DC +2)

Intent: Mild unfriendliness; unhelpful but won't go out of their way

- Merchant: Charges higher prices, doesn't offer their best goods, service is slow and begrudging

- City Guard: Makes you take off your shoes "for inspection," checks your papers twice, generally suspicious

- General NPC: Short answers, no eye contact, clearly wants the interaction to end

- DM Guidance: The NPC finds you annoying or unpleasant but is bound by duty or social norms. They do the minimum required and hope you leave soon

9 to 13: Indifferent (DC 0)

Intent: Neutral; treats you like any other stranger

- Merchant: Standard prices, standard service, nothing special

- City Guard: Basic politeness, does their job by the book, neither helps nor hinders

- General NPC: Polite but distant, transactional interactions only

- DM Guidance: This is the baseline. The NPC has no particular feelings about you. They'll follow standard protocols and social norms. You get what everyone else gets

14 to 16: Friendly (DC -2)

Intent: Warm and welcoming; willing to be helpful within reason

- Merchant: Fair prices, points out good deals, makes pleasant conversation

- City Guard: Addresses you respectfully ("Sir/Madam"), gives directions helpfully, trusts at face value

- General NPC: Smiles, makes eye contact, willing to chat, remembers your name

- DM Guidance: The NPC likes you and shows it through small courtesies. They're not going out of their way, but they're genuinely pleased to interact with you. They'll give benefit of the doubt

17 to 19: Like (DC -5)

Intent: Genuinely fond of you; willing to bend minor rules or go out of their way

- Merchant: Gives discounts, offers extra samples, saves good items for you

- City Guard: Lets you skip lines, overlooks very minor infractions, gives helpful warnings

- General NPC: Invites you for a drink, offers unsolicited help, shares gossip/information

- Guidance: The NPC considers you a friend or at least someone they're happy to see. They'll do favors that don't seriously compromise them. They trust your word over strangers'

20 to 22: Fascinated (DC -10)

Intent: Strongly attracted to you (not necessarily romantic); eager to please

- Merchant: Significant discounts, gives insider information, prioritizes your orders

- City Guard: Believes your explanations without evidence, actively helps you, bends rules significantly

- General NPC: Seeks your company, does favors without being asked, shares secrets

- Guide: The NPC is captivated by you - your charm, reputation, or something about you has made a strong impression. They want your approval and will take risks for you, though they still have self-preservation instincts

23 to 25: Love (DC -15)

Intent: Deep affection or admiration; willing to take significant personal risk

- Merchant: Gives items at cost or free, offers credit, shares business secrets

- City Guard: Covers for you on minor crimes, warns you of danger, may falsify reports

- General NPC: Completely trusts you, offers their home/resources, puts your needs above theirs

- DM Guidance: This is profound attachment. The NPC would sacrifice comfort and some safety for you. However, they're not mindless - they won't throw their life away or betray their core values (though these may be tested). Use this sparingly

26+: Obsessed (DC -20)

Intent: All-consuming devotion; boundaries are blurred or gone

- Merchant: Gives you whatever you want, may neglect business for you, loans significant sums

- City Guard: Will lie, hide evidence, or look the other way on serious crimes (not murder, but close)

- General NPC: Organizes their life around you, makes extreme sacrifices, may become possessive

- Guide: This is dangerous territory. The NPC's judgment is compromised by their feelings. This can be uncomfortable and should be handled carefully. They might:

- Become jealous or possessive

- Make terrible decisions that endanger themselves

- Refuse to see your flaws

- Ignore warning signs or danger

- This is NOT mind control - they still have core beliefs, but those beliefs are now filtered through their obsession with you

Important: An obsessed evil NPC is still evil. They might want to corrupt you, possess you, or destroy what you love to keep you. An obsessed good NPC might become a liability through their devotion

Group Reactions:

When dealing with a group, either:

- Roll once for the group's general reaction

- Roll for the leader and have others follow suit with small variations

- Roll individually only if the group has strong personalities
